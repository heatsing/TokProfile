# TokLens User-First Editorial Plan

**Status:** Approved planning framework; individual articles still require research and editorial review  
**Market:** United States, United Kingdom, Canada, Australia and English-speaking Europe  
**Language:** Global English; add locale notes only when facts genuinely differ  
**Primary user:** Independent TikTok creator  
**Acquisition users:** Engagement calculator users and people researching public TikTok visibility  
**Future paid user:** Marketer or manager working with creator-authorized data

This plan contains 30 editorial briefs. It is not authorization to automatically generate or publish 30 articles.

---

## 1. Editorial operating model

Content starts with a user task, not a keyword.

Every article must help a defined user move through this sequence:

> Trigger → Required input → Action → Reliable result → Limitation → Next decision

### Required brief fields

Every brief must contain:

- **User:** The specific person the article serves.
- **Trigger:** Why the person needs help now.
- **Task:** What the reader must be able to do after reading.
- **Search intent:** The search behavior being satisfied.
- **Primary keyword:** One primary query, without forcing exact-match repetition.
- **Title:** A direct English-market working title.
- **Outline:** The minimum information needed to complete the task.
- **Evidence:** Primary sources, disclosed product methodology or authorized examples.
- **CTA:** A product action that genuinely matches the task.
- **Readiness:** Whether the content can be published with the current product.

### Readiness states

| State           | Meaning                                                                                      | Publishing rule                                |
| --------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| **Ready**       | The article can satisfy its intent and its CTA works today                                   | Research, review and publish individually      |
| **Conditional** | The topic is valuable, but its primary product CTA depends on a named capability             | Do not publish until the condition is verified |
| **Hold**        | Publishing now would attract an intent the product cannot satisfy or create unnecessary risk | Keep as research backlog only                  |

### Evidence standard

Every article must pass:

1. Manual search-results review.
2. Primary-source research.
3. Product capability check.
4. Privacy, safety or legal review when relevant.
5. Originality and duplication review.
6. Final fact check with a visible reviewed date.

Preferred sources are TikTok Help Center, TikTok for Developers and TikTok Business documentation. Secondary sources may add context but cannot override a current primary source.

Do not invent:

- Search volume.
- Platform behavior.
- Benchmarks presented as universal standards.
- User results or case studies.
- Legal conclusions.
- Accounts, metrics or screenshots presented as real.

## 2. Content journey and product handoff

| User stage | Reader question                                    | Content responsibility                  | Product handoff                  |
| ---------- | -------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| Understand | What does this metric, setting or permission mean? | Define terms and boundaries             | Methodology or calculator        |
| Prepare    | What data, URL or permission do I need?            | Provide a checklist                     | Relevant input or connect screen |
| Complete   | How do I perform the task?                         | Give current, verifiable steps          | Working tool action              |
| Interpret  | What does the result mean?                         | Explain formula, sample and limitations | Result page                      |
| Decide     | What should I do next?                             | Offer a bounded decision framework      | Snapshot, comparison or review   |
| Return     | Why should I repeat this?                          | Explain changes over time               | Historical analytics             |

## 3. Publication sequence

### Wave 1 — Publishable acquisition and trust content

Publish Calculator, methodology and carefully sourced privacy content first. These topics can complete the reader's task without pretending that unavailable Viewer or Downloader functionality exists.

### Wave 2 — Authorized creator onboarding

Publish only after production OAuth, permissions, first snapshot and disconnect/delete paths are verified.

### Wave 3 — Creator decisions and retention

Publish only after at least two snapshots, comparable post windows and the relevant analytics views work with real authorized accounts.

### Wave 4 — Authorized marketer workflows

Publish when the product can invite or manage multiple consenting creators, or when the article intentionally ends at the currently working Calculator.

### Wave 5 — Public media utilities

Publish Viewer and Downloader task content only after each tool returns a real result, has measured failure states and passes its security review.

## Cluster A — Calculate and interpret engagement

These briefs acquire creators and marketers through a task the current Calculator can complete.

### 01. Followers vs views formula

- **Category:** TikTok Analytics
- **User:** Creator or marketer holding public post counts.
- **Trigger:** Two tools produced different engagement-rate results.
- **Task:** Select a denominator and calculate a reproducible estimate.
- **Search intent:** Informational and calculation.
- **Primary keyword:** `TikTok engagement rate formula`
- **Title:** TikTok Engagement Rate Formula: Followers vs Views Explained
- **Outline:** Define interactions; show follower-based and view-based formulas; explain which question each answers; work one clearly labelled illustrative calculation; cover zero and missing values; require the same denominator and sample when comparing accounts.
- **Evidence:** Published TokLens formula; current TikTok metric definitions where available; arithmetic independently checked.
- **CTA:** Calculate a follower-based estimate with the Engagement Rate Calculator.
- **Readiness:** **Ready — P0**

### 02. Why calculators disagree

- **Evidence:** Disclosed equations and test calculations; no competitor accuracy claims without reproducible evidence.
- **CTA:** Recalculate both cases with the same inputs in the Calculator.
- **Readiness:** **Ready — P0**

## 6. Internal linking rules

Every article should have:

1. One primary tool or methodology CTA.
2. One prerequisite article.
3. One interpretation or next-decision article.
4. No link to an unavailable tool presented as operational.

### Preferred paths

- Engagement formula → Calculator → Why calculators disagree → Engagement is not enough.
- Public information → Public/private accounts → Profile view history → Privacy methodology.
- Connect permissions → First snapshot → Metrics glossary → Compare snapshots.
- Posting frequency → Cadence experiment → Weekly review.
- Top posts → Viral or repeatable → Fair comparison → Content audit.
- Creator evaluation → Request analytics access → Same-window comparison → Report labelling.

Avoid inserting the same exact-match anchor across every article. Use descriptive links that explain the next task.

## 7. Content quality gates

An article cannot move to `published: true` until all applicable boxes are checked:

- [ ] The target user and trigger are visible in the introduction.
- [ ] The article answers the query before background explanation.
- [ ] Instructions have been manually verified on the review date.
- [ ] Every formula shows inputs, denominator and sample rules.
- [ ] Estimated values are labelled as estimated.
- [ ] Public and creator-authorized data are clearly separated.
- [ ] Limitations and unavailable cases are included.
- [ ] The CTA completes the same task described by the article.
- [ ] No unsupported anonymity, real-time, private-access or causality claim appears.
- [ ] Examples are authorized, owned or explicitly hypothetical.
- [ ] The article adds information beyond current search-result summaries.
- [ ] Author, reviewer, published date and updated date are present.
- [ ] A refresh owner and review interval are assigned.

## 8. Measurement

Traffic is a diagnostic metric, not the final success metric.

### Per-article metrics

- Search query matches the intended user task.
- Scroll or interaction reaches the task answer.
- CTA start rate.
- Downstream tool completion rate.
- Return-to-content rate for the next task.
- Support and failed-input reduction after publication.

### Cluster metrics

| Cluster              | Primary outcome                                               |

| Engagement           | Completed calculations and consistent inputs                  |
