A creator with the highest follower count or engagement-rate estimate is not automatically the best creator for a campaign.

Responsible evaluation starts with the campaign decision, applies one measurement method to a disclosed sample, reviews the content itself and asks the creator for first-party context when public information cannot answer the question.

If you are deciding which evidence must sit beside the percentage, start with [When TikTok Engagement Rate Is Not Enough](/blog/when-tiktok-engagement-rate-is-not-enough).

Use the checklist below for an initial review. It is not a fraud detector, legal opinion or substitute for a conversation with the creator.

## The short checklist

Before recommending a TikTok creator, document:

1. Campaign objective and target audience.
2. Content and brand fit.
3. Account identity and public availability.
4. Comparable post sample.
5. One labelled engagement-rate formula.
6. Performance consistency and outliers.
7. First-party data still needed.
8. Brand-safety and authenticity review.
9. Commercial disclosure readiness.
10. Content rights and operational fit.
11. Known limitations and unknowns.
12. A decision tied to evidence, not a universal score.

An honest `unknown` is more useful than an invented metric.

## 1. Define the campaign question first

Do not begin by sorting a creator list by follower count.

Write the decision the research must support:

- Is the creator relevant to a specific audience?
- Can the creator explain or demonstrate the product credibly?
- Does the campaign need reach, reusable creative, conversions or community discussion?
- Is the content intended for organic publication, paid amplification or both?
- Which countries, languages and age groups matter?
- What result must be measured after publication?

The objective determines which evidence is useful.

For example, a creator can be a strong source of reusable product demonstrations without having the largest public audience. Another creator may provide broad reach but have weak subject relevance.

## 2. Review content fit before metrics

Watch a consistent sample of recent content.

Record:

- Main topics.
- Typical formats.
- Speaking and editing style.
- Product categories already featured.
- Audience questions visible in comments.
- Tone and language.
- Frequency of commercial posts.
- Whether the creator demonstrates real use or repeats generic claims.

Content fit cannot be reduced to a follower count.

Ask whether the proposed product belongs naturally in the creator's existing content. A sudden, unexplained category change may feel less credible than a collaboration connected to an established topic.

Do not infer audience demographics from appearance, language or a few comments. Request first-party audience information when demographics are material to the campaign.

## 3. Confirm identity and public availability

Record the exact:

- Username.
- Official profile URL.
- Display name.
- Verification status, if visible and relevant.
- Public/private state.
- Collection date.

Usernames and profile links can change. Preserve the reviewed URL and date in the research record.

If a profile field or post is unavailable, label it unavailable. Do not convert missing information to zero or assume the account deleted the content.

For public-data boundaries, read [What Information Is Public on a TikTok Profile?](/blog/what-information-is-public-on-tiktok).

## 4. Select one comparable post sample

Define the sample before calculating a rate.

A sample description should include:

- Number of posts.
- Earliest and latest publication dates.
- Data collection date.
- Post-age rule.
- Treatment of pinned posts.
- Treatment of promoted or branded content.
- Excluded or unavailable posts.
- Formats included.

Use the same rules for every creator in the shortlist.

Avoid comparing one creator's latest five posts with another creator's best five posts. Avoid mixing a two-hour-old post with mature posts without noting the age difference.

If one viral post dominates the account, show the result with and without that post rather than silently removing it.

The [3, 5 and 10-post sample comparison](/blog/how-sample-size-changes-tiktok-engagement-rate) shows how one valid breakout post can move the mean, median and two engagement-rate formulas in different directions.

## 5. Calculate one transparent engagement estimate

Choose the denominator from the question.

### Follower-based estimate

`(Average likes + Average comments + Average shares) ÷ Followers × 100`

This is useful for a consistent high-level comparison when the same sample and interaction types are used for every account.

### View-based estimate

`(Total likes + Total comments + Total shares) ÷ Total views × 100`

This describes visible interactions relative to views across the sampled posts.

Always label:

- Denominator.
- Included interactions.
- Post count.
- Sample dates.
- Collection date.
- Missing fields.
- Estimated status.

The [TokLens TikTok Engagement Rate Calculator](/tiktok-engagement-rate-calculator) calculates the follower-based estimate from values you enter and displays the formula beside the result.

If two tools disagree, [audit their formulas and samples](/blog/why-tiktok-engagement-calculators-differ) before selecting a number.

## 6. Check consistency and outliers

One average can hide very different performance patterns.

Inspect:

- Range of views and interactions.
- Median as well as mean, when possible.
- Number of posts above and below the baseline.
- Whether one post contributes most of the total.
- Changes in posting frequency.
- Differences between commercial and non-commercial content.
- Whether recent results are mature enough to compare.

Do not label a high-performing post a repeatable pattern until similar content has produced comparable results more than once.

Do not treat lower variance as automatically better. A campaign may value creative experimentation. The objective determines how consistency should be interpreted.

## 7. Separate public estimates from authorized data

Public information can support an initial shortlist. It cannot answer every campaign question.

### Public or externally observable context may include:

- Public profile identity.
- Public posts.
- Visible views, likes, comments and shares when available.
- Posting dates.
- Public content themes.
- Commercial-content labels.

### Creator-authorized or creator-provided context may include:

- Audience countries and demographics.
- Watch time and retention.
- Traffic or conversion results.
- Detailed campaign performance.
- Organic versus paid context.
- Content-level insights shared through an authorized brand workflow.

TikTok's Display API requires the relevant user authorization and scopes. A marketer should not describe creator-authorized data as if it were available for every public competitor.

Ask only for information needed for the campaign decision. Define:

- Purpose.
- Date range.
- Who will access it.
- How long it will be retained.
- When it will be deleted.

Screenshots without a visible account, date range or source may require clarification.

## 8. Review brand safety manually

Brand safety is contextual and cannot be delegated to one numeric score.

Review a disclosed sample for:

- Topics that conflict with the brand's policy.
- Misleading product or health claims.
- Harassment or discriminatory content.
- Undisclosed commercial relationships.
- Repeated copyright concerns.
- Artificial-looking comment patterns that require clarification.
- Sudden topic or account-identity changes.
- Existing competitor relationships.

Use TikTok's current Community Guidelines and the brand's own policy.

Do not accuse a creator of fake engagement from a public ratio alone. Unusual patterns are a reason to ask for context, not proof of misconduct.

Record the reviewed posts and date so another reviewer can reproduce the assessment.

## 9. Check commercial disclosure readiness

TikTok currently requires creators promoting a brand, product or service to turn on its commercial content disclosure setting.

Depending on the relationship, TikTok may label content:

- `Promotional content`.
- `Paid partnership`.

TikTok says a creator can tag a brand partner. That workflow can support discovery, advertising authorization and access to asset-level post insights through Content Suite.

Before contracting, confirm:

- Who is responsible for enabling the TikTok disclosure.
- Which label applies.
- Whether the brand must be tagged.
- Whether paid amplification is planned.
- Which market-specific disclosure language is required.
- Who checks the final post before publication.

Platform tools do not replace applicable law.

For U.S.-directed endorsements, the Federal Trade Commission says material relationships should be disclosed clearly and in a place people will notice. In the UK, ASA/CAP guidance also emphasizes making ads recognizable as ads. Requirements can differ across the United States, United Kingdom, Canada, Australia and Europe.

Use qualified legal or compliance review for the target market. This checklist is general product and research guidance, not legal advice.

## 10. Clarify content and advertising rights

Publishing permission, content ownership and advertising authorization are different decisions.

Document:

- Organic publication account.
- Campaign dates.
- Draft and revision process.
- Usage duration.
- Countries and channels.
- Editing rights.
- Paid-media rights.
- Spark Ads or other advertising authorization.
- Whitelisting or account-access expectations.
- Exclusivity.
- Music and third-party asset restrictions.
- Required removal or archive process.

Do not assume a public TikTok post can be downloaded, edited and used in advertising.

TikTok's brand-partner workflow may make advertising authorization easier, but the campaign agreement still needs to describe the intended use.

## 11. Evaluate operational fit

A creator can fit the audience and still be a poor operational match for a particular campaign.

Confirm:

- Availability.
- Production timeline.
- Response expectations.
- Required approvals.
- Number of concepts and revisions.
- Product-shipping constraints.
- Disclosure requirements.
- Reporting deadline.
- Preferred payment process.
- Contact and escalation route.

Ask for relevant examples of previous campaign workflow, not confidential details from another client.

Operational clarity protects both the marketer and creator.

## 12. Keep unknowns visible

Create three evidence states:

- **Verified:** supported by a disclosed source.
- **Creator-provided:** supplied by the creator and labelled accordingly.
- **Unknown:** not available or not yet requested.

Do not create a zero for an unknown field.

Do not average public estimates and first-party campaign metrics into one unexplained score.

An evaluation record should allow another reviewer to see:

- What was observed.
- What was calculated.
- What was supplied.
- What remains unknown.
- Why the recommendation follows.

## A decision record without a fake universal score

Use a short decision record instead of a mysterious 0–100 rating.

### Campaign

- Objective.
- Audience.
- Market.
- Content deliverable.

### Evidence

- Content-fit observations.
- Public sample and dates.
- Engagement formula and result.
- Authorized or creator-provided context.
- Brand-safety review.
- Disclosure and rights status.

### Decision

- Advance.
- Hold for more information.
- Do not advance for this campaign.

### Reason

Write two or three evidence-based sentences tied to the objective.

A “do not advance” decision should describe campaign mismatch, not declare that the creator has no general value.

## Three illustrative scenarios

### High engagement, low campaign fit

A creator has a high follower-based estimate, but recent content focuses on a topic unrelated to the product. The visible audience discussion also centers on that unrelated topic.

Decision: hold or decline for this campaign because relevance is unsupported. The percentage does not solve the fit problem.

### Moderate estimate, strong content fit

A creator has a moderate estimate, consistently publishes credible demonstrations in the target category and can provide relevant audience and retention context.

Decision: advance to a conversation. The evaluation uses quantitative and qualitative evidence.

### One viral post dominates the average

A creator's latest sample contains one unusually large post. Other posts have a much lower baseline.

Decision: show both the complete sample and the result without the outlier, then ask whether the successful format has been repeated. Do not present the viral result as guaranteed performance.

All scenarios are illustrative and are not benchmark recommendations.
